Research-Creation for Critical Digital Literacy

Over two decades of making documentaries about the internet, I've watched remix culture go from radical creative act to the default logic of digital life. What happens when the tools that once helped us critique mass media become the very mechanisms that shape—and sometimes destabilize—our shared reality?

This dissertation explores how interactive documentary can help us not just understand the internet, but imagine it otherwise. Through three playful, speculative projects—an Instagram face filter, an AI-powered futures game, and a motion-controlled arcade experience—I investigate how we might cultivate critically literate digital publics capable of both questioning extractive systems and rehearsing alternatives.

The work sits at the intersection of documentary, speculative design, culture jamming, and game design. It's rooted in a simple conviction: that the way we build literacy about digital systems should itself be joyful, embodied, and imaginative.
